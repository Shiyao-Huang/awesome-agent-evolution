\section{Evolutionary Systems for Self-Improving AI}
\label{ch:systems}

\subsection{Introduction and Motivation}

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) as general-purpose reasoning and code generation engines has catalyzed a paradigm shift in optimization, algorithm design, and system improvement. A new class of systems treats the LLM itself as an evolutionary operator---a mechanism that proposes, refines, and selects candidate solutions iteratively. This chapter analyzes the key systems exemplifying this paradigm across three perspectives: LLM-driven optimization, evolutionary code discovery, and agent-level self-evolution.

These systems share a \textbf{Generate--Evaluate--Select} loop: at each iteration, a generator (LLM) proposes candidates; an evaluator assigns scores; a selection mechanism retains the best. What distinguishes them from classical evolutionary computation is the LLM's role: rather than applying fixed mutation operators, the LLM leverages parametric knowledge to propose semantically meaningful changes, conducting \emph{informed search} over programs, prompts, or architectures.

\paragraph{Historical Context.}
Neural Architecture Search demonstrated that network architectures could be discovered rather than designed. AutoML automated pipeline configuration. Quality-Diversity algorithms showed how to maintain diverse high-performing populations. The systems in this chapter build on these foundations but introduce a transformative element: the LLM as an intelligent mutation operator whose semantically informed proposals dramatically accelerate search.

\paragraph{The Evolutionary Intelligence Paradigm.}
A unifying tension is \emph{exploration vs.\ exploitation}. Classical EAs address this through fitness sharing, niching, and island models. The surveyed systems inherit these but augment them with LLM-specific capabilities: reasoning about \emph{why} a change might help, incorporating pre-training knowledge, and generating novel solutions unlikely under random mutation. This combination represents the \textbf{Evolutionary Intelligence} paradigm---a key pillar of self-evolving AI.


\subsection{LLM as Optimizer}
\label{sec:ch4-llm-optimizer}

\subsubsection{OPRO: Optimization by PROmpting}

OPRO~\citep{yang2023opro} (Google DeepMind) establishes that LLMs can serve as general-purpose optimizers when problems are expressed in natural language. The key innovation is the \textbf{meta-prompt}: presenting the LLM with solution--score history and asking for higher-scoring solutions.

\paragraph{Algorithmic Framework.}
Given objective $f$ and history $\mathcal{H}=\{(s_i, f(s_i))\}$, the meta-prompt $\mathcal{M}$ formats pairs in ascending score order (best last):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M} = \text{Format}\left(\mathcal{H}, \text{task\_description}\right)
\end{equation}
The optimizer generates $m$ candidates at temperature 1.0, each evaluated by a zero-temperature scorer, with top-$K$ history update:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H} \leftarrow \text{TopK}\left(\mathcal{H} \cup \{(s'_j, f(s'_j))\}_{j=1}^{m}, K\right), \quad K=20, m=8
\end{equation}
This repeats for $T=200$ iterations.

\paragraph{Key Results.}
OPRO discovered ``\textit{Take a deep breath and work on this problem step by step}'' achieves \textbf{80\% on GSM8K} (from 34\% baseline) with PaLM 2---one of the most cited findings in prompt optimization. This transformed prompt engineering from a manual art to automated optimization.

\paragraph{Design Decisions.}
(1) Score bucketization: continuous scores discretized into 100 buckets; (2) Error-driven few-shot: system tracks frequently misclassified examples as few-shot context; (3) Multi-position placement: four instruction positions evaluated. OPRO also generalizes to linear regression and TSP, demonstrating the paradigm's breadth.

\paragraph{Scaling Analysis.}
Cost is $T \times m \times |\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}|$ LLM inferences---hundreds of thousands for GSM8K. Quality depends on optimizer LLM capability, improving with model scale but remaining imperfect.


\subsubsection{EvoPrompt: Evolutionary Prompt Optimization}

EvoPrompt~\citep{guo2023evoprompt} (ICLR 2024) frames prompt optimization as evolutionary computation, instantiating GA and DE with LLMs as crossover/mutation operators. GA uses tournament selection, LLM-guided crossover of two parents, and elitist replacement. DE uses greedy replacement: trial replaces current only if scoring higher. A dual-model architecture separates the evolution model from the task model.

Evaluated on 31 datasets (classification, summarization, BBH reasoning): up to \textbf{25\% improvement} over human-designed prompts on BBH, consistent superiority over APE and LM-BFF. GA outperforms DE on classification; DE competitive on generation.

\paragraph{Transition to Code Evolution.}
EvoPrompt's insight---LLMs as intelligent crossover/mutation operators in language space---generalizes to code space, where formal syntax and semantics enable more powerful operations. This transition is the subject of the next section.


\subsection{LLM as Evolutionary Operator: Code and Algorithm Discovery}
\label{sec:ch4-evo-operator}

\subsubsection{FunSearch: Mathematical Discovery via Program Search}

FunSearch~\citep{romera2024funsearch} (Google DeepMind, \textit{Nature} 2023) is the first demonstration that LLMs can make \emph{new mathematical discoveries}. Rather than searching for solutions, it searches the space of \emph{programs that generate solutions}.

\paragraph{Architecture.}
Four parallel components: \textbf{ProgramsDatabase} (island-model: Database $\rightarrow$ Island $\rightarrow$ Cluster $\rightarrow$ Program), \textbf{Sampler} (parent selection + prompt construction), \textbf{LLM} (4 candidates per prompt), \textbf{Evaluator} (sandbox execution + scoring).

\paragraph{Island Model with Signature Clustering.}
10 independent islands with signature-based clustering (programs grouped by per-test-case score tuples). Selection uses temperature-annealed softmax:
\begin{equation}
P(c) = \frac{\exp(\text{score}(c)/\tau)}{\sum_{c'}\exp(\text{score}(c')/\tau)}, \quad \tau_0=0.1
\end{equation}
Bottom 50\% islands reset every 14,400s with top-island best programs (migration).

\paragraph{Prompt Engineering.}
Versioned program chains: \texttt{v0} (low score) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{v1} (medium) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{v2} (empty, LLM completes). Progressive docstrings (``Improved version of\ldots'') provide evolutionary narrative. Three-level safety filtering: execution success (30s timeout), ancestor-call detection, return-type validation.

\paragraph{Key Discoveries.}
New cap set bounds for $n\geq 8$ in $\mathbb{F}_3^n$ (first improvement in 20+ years); improved bin-packing heuristics beating FFD/BFD; new cyclic graph independent sets. Default config: 15 samplers, 140 evaluators (1:9 ratio), multi-day runs.

\paragraph{Computational Cost.}
Estimated several hundred thousand LLM calls per discovery. The 1:9 sampler-to-evaluator ratio indicates evaluation is the primary bottleneck. Scaling analysis reveals evaluation speed, not generation diversity, fundamentally limits performance.


\subsubsection{AlphaEvolve: Gemini-Powered Algorithm Discovery}

AlphaEvolve~\citep{novikov2025alphaevolve} extends FunSearch from single-function to whole-program optimization via \textbf{dual-model design}: Gemini Flash (breadth---many diverse candidates) + Gemini Pro (depth---targeted improvements).

\paragraph{MAP-Elites Quality-Diversity.}
\begin{equation}
\text{Archive}[\mathbf{b}(p)] \leftarrow p \quad \text{if } f(p) > f(\text{Archive}[\mathbf{b}(p)])
\end{equation}
Behavioral descriptors are problem-specific (multiplications, memory accesses, throughput). Adaptive evolution context learns from search history.

\paragraph{Results.}
$4\times4$ complex matrix multiplication: \textbf{48 multiplications} (first Strassen improvement in 56 years); Borg scheduling: \textbf{0.7\%} worldwide compute recovery; FlashAttention: \textbf{23\% speedup}; attention: \textbf{32\% speedup}; math: SOTA for 75\% of 50+ problems, better for 20\%.

\paragraph{Comparison with FunSearch.}
Three extensions: (1) single-function $\rightarrow$ whole-program; (2) single LLM $\rightarrow$ dual-model; (3) static $\rightarrow$ adaptive context. Costs: requires multiple LLM tiers and sophisticated evaluation infrastructure. Not publicly available.


\subsubsection{ReEvo: Reflective Evolution}

ReEvo~\citep{ye2024reevo} (NeurIPS 2024) introduces \textbf{dual-layer reflection}: short-term (analyzes specific failures) + long-term (accumulates cross-iteration knowledge):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{\text{long}}^{(t)} = \text{LLM}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\text{long}}^{(t-1)}, \{\text{results at iteration } t\}\right)
\end{equation}
Supports different LLMs per operator. On TSP, CVRP, OP, MKP, BPP: beats human-designed heuristics. Ablation confirms both reflection layers critical.


\subsubsection{OpenELM: Open-Source Evolution}

OpenELM~\citep{carperai2023openelm} provides open-source MAP-Elites + LLM with four-layer design (ELM $\rightarrow$ MAP-Elites $\rightarrow$ Environment $\rightarrow$ MutationModel). Three QD algorithms (standard, CVT, Deep Grid). Diverse environments (Sodaracer, images, puzzles, poetry). gVisor sandbox for safe execution.

\paragraph{Open-Ended Archive.}
Monotonically growing archive $\mathcal{A}$ preserves \emph{stepping stones}. Sampling: $P(a_i) \propto f(a_i) \times \exp(-\beta \cdot \text{similarity}(a_i, \mathcal{A}))$. Modification: $a_{\text{child}} = \text{LLM}(\text{code}(a_p), \text{edit}, \text{history})$. Child added only if improving on $\geq1$ benchmark.

\paragraph{Results.}
SWE-bench: \textbf{20.0\%$\rightarrow$50.0\%} (+150\%); Polyglot: \textbf{14.2\%$\rightarrow$30.7\%} (+116\%). Novel strategies discovered. Cross-benchmark transfer confirmed. Safety concerns: arbitrary self-modification requires constrained generation and formal verification for deployment.


\subsubsection{ADAS: Automated Design of Agentic Systems}

ADAS~\citep{hu2024adas} (ICLR 2025) searches Turing-complete Python code space for agent architectures. Meta-agent iteratively writes, evaluates, and refines architectures. Strong \textbf{cross-domain transfer}: ARC$\rightarrow$GFootball; GPT-3.5 designs effective on GPT-4. Learns general design principles, not task-specific overfitting.


\subsubsection{G\"{o}del Agent: Runtime Self-Modification}

G\"{o}del Agent~\citep{yin2025godelagent} uses Python monkey patching for in-place self-modification: introspection $\rightarrow$ LLM proposes changes $\rightarrow$ \texttt{exec()} applies $\rightarrow$ validate with rollback. Lighter than DGM but less exploration diversity. HotPotQA +8--15\%; ALFWorld significant improvement. Polaris follow-up extends to smaller models.


\subsubsection{Agent Symbolic Learning}

Agent Symbolic Learning~\citep{zhou2024symbolic} (NeurIPS 2024) treats agents as symbolic networks (prompts $\mathcal{P}$, tools $\mathcal{T}$, pipelines $\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{O}$) and performs backpropagation in language space:
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{\text{lang}}^{(n)} = \text{LLM}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{gradient}}(\nabla_{\text{lang}}^{(n+1)}, \text{node}_n, \mathcal{L}_{\text{lang}}))
\end{equation}
Three optimizers: PromptOptimizer, ToolOptimizer, PipelineOptimizer. Results: HotPotQA F1 36.2$\rightarrow$39.1; MATH 56.0\%$\rightarrow$60.7\%; HumanEval 68.3\%$\rightarrow$73.2\%. Outperforms DSPy on all benchmarks.


\subsubsection{SelfEvolve}

SelfEvolve~\citep{jiang2023selfevolve}: two-stage pipeline. Stage 1: self-generated knowledge $p(K)=\prod_i p_\theta(K_i|X,K_{<i})$. Stage 2: iterative refinement with execution feedback. HumanEval 74.39\%$\rightarrow$85.98\%; DS-1000 49.4$\rightarrow$57.2.


\subsubsection{Absolute Zero}

Absolute Zero~\citep{zhao2025absolutezero} (Tsinghua, NeurIPS 2025): single model as task proposer + solver. $R_{\text{proposer}} \propto \text{difficulty}(t)\times\text{solvability}(t)$. AZR-Coder-7B: 7B SOTA on HumanEval+/MBPP+ with zero external data. Code self-play transfers to math reasoning.


\subsubsection{Reflexion and Voyager}

\textbf{Reflexion}~\citep{shinn2023reflexion}: verbal reinforcement learning with episodic memory. Actor-Evaluator-Reflection loop. \textbf{91\% pass@1 HumanEval} (surpassing GPT-4's 80\%). AlfWorld 97\% vs 75\% baseline. Limitation: unbounded memory growth.

\textbf{Voyager}~\citep{wang2023voyager}: Minecraft lifelong learning via automatic curriculum + skill library + iterative prompting. \textbf{3.3$\times$ more unique items, 15.3$\times$ faster milestones}. Skills accumulate as building blocks.


\subsubsection{Self-Rewarding Language Models}

Self-Rewarding LM~\citep{meta2024selfrewarding}: unified policy + reward model with Iterative DPO. Meta-Rewarding adds meta-reward evaluation. Llama-3-8B: AlpacaEval 2.0 \textbf{22.9\%$\rightarrow$39.4\%}. Limitations: length bias, reward hacking. Bridges evolutionary systems (this chapter) and training-time methods (Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}).


\subsection{Open-Source Implementations}
\label{sec:ch4-opensource}

\subsubsection{LLaMEA}

LLaMEA~\citep{chen2025llamea} (IEEE TEVC 2025, GECCO Silver Humies): open-source AlphaEvolve successor. Features: (1) LLaMEA-HPO: SMAC-based hyperparameter optimization, 3--5$\times$ cost reduction; (2) Diff-mode: SEARCH/REPLACE patches, 10--20\% token use; (3) Multi-objective: Pareto archive; (4) Code evolution graphs. Supports OpenAI, Gemini, Ollama.

\subsubsection{OpenTreeSearch}

OpenTreeSearch~\citep{genentech2025opentreesearch}: PUCT tree search with single hyperparameter $C$:
\begin{equation}
\text{PUCT} = \text{score}(\text{child}) + C\sqrt{\ln(\text{parent.visits})/\text{child.visits}}
\end{equation}
Virtual loss for parallelism. Circle packing: 2.632 in 121 iter vs AlphaEvolve 2.635 (200 iter). HTML visualization + LLM summaries.

\subsubsection{Evolution Target Taxonomy}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Prompt level} (OPRO, EvoPrompt): discrete, easy to evaluate; limited scope.
    \item \textbf{Code level} (FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, ReEvo, OpenTreeSearch): arbitrary algorithms; needs automated evaluators.
    \item \textbf{Algorithm level} (LLaMEA): must generalize across instances; expensive evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Agent level} (DGM, ADAS, G\"{o}del Agent): most ambitious; multi-task; potentially unsafe.
\end{enumerate}

Progression: peripheral (prompts) $\rightarrow$ core (code, algorithms, architectures) $\rightarrow$ fully self-improving AI.

\subsubsection{The Evaluation Bottleneck}

The evaluator is the critical bottleneck across all systems. FunSearch: 140 evaluators per 15 samplers. AlphaEvolve: relies on domain-specific evaluators. DGM: depends on standardized benchmarks. Self-constructing evaluators (Absolute Zero direction) are the most promising solution to this fundamental limitation.

\subsubsection{Scaling Laws}

\paragraph{Iteration Scaling.} Performance follows logarithmic scaling: rapid initial improvement, then diminishing returns. Most breakthroughs occur in the first 25\% of iterations.

\paragraph{LLM Capability Scaling.} DGM reports GPT-4/Claude significantly outperforms GPT-3.5 for code modification quality. Relationship appears roughly linear: LLM benchmark improvement $X$\% yields $\sim X$\% evolution convergence improvement. As foundation models improve, self-evolution automatically benefits---current results represent a floor, not ceiling.

\paragraph{Evaluation Budget.} FunSearch's 1:9 ratio indicates evaluation costs 9$\times$ more than generation. For real-world interaction domains (robotics, experiments), this ratio becomes extreme, motivating surrogate models and active evaluation.

\subsubsection{Cross-Cutting Observations}

\paragraph{1. LLM Quality $>$ Search Sophistication.}
FunSearch (9+ params) $\approx$ OpenTreeSearch (1 param) on shared benchmarks. Better LLMs yield higher returns than complex algorithms.

\paragraph{2. Transfer is Key.}
ADAS shows strongest cross-domain transfer. Search cost amortizes across applications. Overfitting to specific benchmarks limits practical value.

\paragraph{3. Safety Scales with Target.}
Prompts ($\approx$ no risk) to agent architectures ($\approx$ high risk). Formal verification and alignment-aware evaluation are urgent.

\paragraph{4. Open-Source Convergence.}
LLaMEA $\approx$ AlphaEvolve; OpenTreeSearch matches with simpler search; ReEvo adds reflection. Core ideas understood; future from applications/scaling.

\paragraph{5. Evaluation is the True Bottleneck.}
All impressive results in domains with automated evaluation. Extending to domains without clear criteria remains open.


\subsubsection{The Role of Foundation Models}

A critical factor is LLM backend choice. Empirical patterns: (1) more capable models propose higher-quality mutations; (2) the gap narrows for well-structured spaces and widens for open-ended ones; (3) code-specialized models outperform general-purpose at similar size; (4) cost--performance favors cheap models for breadth (Flash) and expensive for depth (Pro). This four-point analysis suggests that as foundation models improve, self-evolution quality will improve proportionally---making current results a lower bound on future performance.


\subsubsection{Future Directions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Self-constructing evaluators}: Systems that build their own evaluation functions. Absolute Zero's self-play is an early step. Meta-evaluation frameworks that bootstrap evaluation quality would expand self-evolution to creative writing, strategic planning, and social interaction domains.

    \item \textbf{Hierarchical evolution}: Simultaneous evolution at multiple levels (prompts, code, architectures) with cross-level information flow. The five-loop integration gap (Table~\ref{tab:loop-mapping}) suggests synergistic improvements from combining all loop types.

    \item \textbf{Sample-efficient evolution}: Bayesian optimization surrogates, active learning, transfer learning to reduce LLM calls from thousands to hundreds. Lightweight ``mutation quality predictors'' could filter candidates before expensive evaluation.

    \item \textbf{Safe self-modification}: Formal verification of self-modifying code, constrained generation, alignment-aware evaluation. ``Constitutional evolution'' frameworks governing evolution by explicit safety principles.

    \item \textbf{Open-ended evolution}: Systems that autonomously discover new tasks, challenges, and evaluation criteria. POET's co-evolution of tasks and agents provides one model. Intrinsic motivation mechanisms for self-directed improvement at the Level 3--4 boundary.
\end{enumerate}
