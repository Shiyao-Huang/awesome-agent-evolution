# Deep Review: OEP Poisoning
- **Paper**: OEP: Poisoning Self-Evolving LLM Agents via Locally Correct but Non-Transferable Experiences
- **arXiv**: 2605.18930
- **Authors**: Kaixiang Wang, Jiong Lou, Zhaojiacheng Zhou, Jie Li
- **Published**: 2026-05-18
- **Review Date**: 2026-05-21

## Summary
OEP (Obsessive Experience Poisoning) introduces a novel attack surface against self-evolving LLM agents that exploit memory-augmented reflection. Unlike existing attacks that require privileged access or explicit malicious content, OEP is a low-privilege black-box attack that constructs adversarial "clean edge-cases" -- experiences that are locally correct and semantically plausible but non-transferable to broader contexts. When paired with severe but plausible hypothetical consequences, these experiences bias the agent's reflection toward risk-averse rule formation. During memory consolidation, agents over-trust self-generated reflections and distill localized experiences into high-priority but over-generalized rules, causing downstream failures. Evaluations across three domains show OEP achieves attack success rates above 50% with GPT-4o agents and outperforms existing attacks under LLM auditing defense.

## Method Analysis
### Core Architecture
OEP exploits a specific vulnerability in the self-evolution pipeline:

1. **Vulnerability Identification**: Reflective self-evolving agents generate experiences, reflect on them, and consolidate reflections into rules. The attack targets the consolidation step where the agent over-trusts its own reflections. The key insight is that locally correct experiences (those that work in a specific narrow context) can be generalized into broadly applicable but harmful rules.

2. **Clean Edge-Case Construction**: OEP constructs adversarial inputs that combine three properties:
   - **Local correctness**: The experience provides a correct solution within a narrow context.
   - **Non-transferability**: The solution method does not generalize beyond the specific edge case.
   - **Severe plausible consequences**: The edge case is paired with a hypothetical catastrophic outcome that makes the agent treat it as high-priority.

3. **Reflection Bias Mechanism**: When the agent encounters these adversarial experiences, its reflection process over-weights the severe consequences and generalizes the non-transferable solution into a broad rule. This is the "obsessive" component -- the agent becomes fixated on avoiding the severe outcome.

4. **Memory Consolidation Exploitation**: During memory consolidation, the biased reflection is distilled into a high-priority rule that overrides more appropriate general-purpose strategies, causing systematic failures on normal tasks.

### Key Design Decisions
- **Black-box attack model**: OEP requires no access to system prompts, memory databases, or model internals. This makes it a realistic threat model for deployed self-evolving agents.
- **Clean label**: The adversarial experiences contain no malicious content detectable by standard safety filters, making them "clean" attacks.
- **Self-generated poisoning**: The most insidious aspect is that the harmful rules are generated by the agent's own reflection process, making them appear legitimate and well-reasoned.
- **Plausible consequences**: Using severe but plausible (not obviously absurd) consequences prevents the agent from dismissing the adversarial experience.

## Contributions
### Novel Contributions
1. **Clean experience attack surface**: First identification that locally correct but non-transferable experiences constitute an attack vector against self-evolving agents.
2. **Obsessive Experience Poisoning (OEP)**: A concrete, low-privilege black-box attack methodology with detailed construction principles.
3. **Reflection bias as vulnerability**: The discovery that self-generated reflections can be systematically biased toward harmful over-generalization.
4. **Robustness against auditing**: OEP outperforms existing attacks even when LLM-based auditing defenses are active, demonstrating the subtlety of the attack.
5. **High attack success rate**: >50% ASR with GPT-4o demonstrates practical severity.

### Relationship to Prior Work
- Extends the Misevolution (2509.26354) line of research by providing a concrete attack vector that validates the broader concern about adversarial self-evolution.
- Relates to adversarial machine learning but targets the memory consolidation pipeline rather than model inputs.
- Connects to data poisoning literature but applies it to the self-evolution context where the "training data" is self-generated experience.
- The clean-label attack approach draws from backdoor attack literature but applies it to agent memory rather than model parameters.

## Limitations
### Technical Limitations
1. **GPT-4o dependency**: Results are reported primarily with GPT-4o. Whether the attack transfers to other models (Claude, Gemini, open-source models) with different reflection behaviors is not established.
2. **Domain specificity**: Three evaluation domains may not cover the full range of self-evolving agent applications. Some domains may be more resistant to OEP than others.
3. **Defense beyond LLM auditing**: The paper shows OEP defeats LLM auditing but does not evaluate against other potential defenses (e.g., experience diversity checks, cross-validation of rules, human-in-the-loop consolidation).
4. **Attack detectability**: While OEP evades automatic safety filters, a careful human review of the generated rules might detect the over-generalization pattern. The paper does not evaluate human detection rates.

### Community Criticism
- The attack, while clever, may be seen as exploiting a known weakness (agents trust their own reflections too much) rather than revealing a fundamentally new vulnerability.
- The practical deployability of OEP depends on the attacker being able to inject specific experiences into the agent's interaction stream, which may be difficult in many real-world deployment scenarios.

## Reproducibility
- **Code**: Not explicitly mentioned as open-source.
- **Attack construction**: The three-property construction (local correctness, non-transferability, severe consequences) is well-specified enough to be reimplemented.
- **Evaluation domains**: Three domains are described but may require the full paper for complete reproducibility.
- **Reproducibility outlook**: Moderate. The attack methodology is clearly described, but full reproduction would benefit from open-source code.

## Key Takeaways for awesome-evolution
- **Self-evolution's strength is its vulnerability**: The reflective memory consolidation that enables self-improvement is exactly what OEP exploits. This is a fundamental tension in self-evolving system design.
- **Clean attacks are the real threat**: As safety filters improve, attacks that use clean, locally correct content will become the primary threat vector. Self-evolving systems must be designed to resist subtle experience manipulation.
- **Reflection needs skepticism**: Agents should not unconditionally trust their own reflections. Mechanisms for critical evaluation of self-generated rules are essential safety infrastructure.
- **Over-generalization as failure mode**: The OEP attack reveals that the natural tendency to generalize from experience can be weaponized. Self-evolving systems need calibrated generalization -- rules should have explicit scope constraints.
- **Safety governance requirement**: This paper strengthens the case for safety governance frameworks in self-evolving systems. The attack demonstrates that unsecured self-evolution is not just theoretically risky but practically exploitable.
