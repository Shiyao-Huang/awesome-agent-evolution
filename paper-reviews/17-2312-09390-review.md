## Limitations and Critical Evaluation

A key limitation is that self-improvement claims often depend heavily on benchmark design, evaluator reliability, and task distribution. If the evaluator is weak or narrow, an agent may learn to exploit the measurement process rather than acquire robust capabilities. The paper should therefore be interpreted through the quality of its evaluation protocol and the diversity of tasks used.

These costs can make the approach difficult to deploy continuously unless the paper provides efficient gating, selective updates, or strong evidence that improvements transfer beyond the immediate benchmark.

## Implications for Agent Evolution

For agent evolution, the paper suggests a design principle: improvement loops must be explicit, measurable, and reversible. A production-grade evolving agent should keep the paper's core mechanism but add safety gates, regression tests, provenance tracking, and rollback when updates reduce performance.

The paper is also useful for dataset construction. Its tasks, metrics, and failure modes can be encoded as evaluation dimensions for future self-evolving agents. If combined with timestamped raw data and longitudinal benchmarks, it can help measure whether agent evolution methods improve over time or simply adapt to a static benchmark.
