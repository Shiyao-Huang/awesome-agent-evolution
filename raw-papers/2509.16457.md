# Implicit Behavioral Alignment of Language Agents in High-Stakes Crowd Simulations

**Authors**: Yunzhe Wang, Gale M. Lucas, Burcin Becerik-Gerber, Volkan Ustun

**Submitted**: 2025-09-19

**Venue**: EMNLP 2025 (Main)

**Code**: none (arXiv only)

## Abstract

Language-driven agents are increasingly used to simulate human behavior in high-stakes settings such as crowd evacuations, but their default behavior often diverges from how real people act under pressure. The paper introduces a behavioral alignment problem for LLM-based crowd simulation and proposes the Persona-to-Emergent-Behavior Alignment (PEBA) formulation together with PersonaEvolve (PEvo), an LLM-based optimizer that iteratively evolves agent personas. PEvo treats the natural-language personas of a crowd of LLM agents as the mutable object, uses the gap between emergent crowd behavior and expert benchmarks as the feedback signal, and asks an optimizer LLM to propose persona revisions that close that gap. Iterating this loop drives the collective behavior of the agent crowd toward realistic, expert-validated dynamics without retraining the underlying model.

## Key Contributions

1. **Persona-to-Emergent-Behavior Alignment (PEBA)**: Frames the problem of making a population of language agents behave realistically as aligning natural-language personas with emergent, expert-benchmarked crowd dynamics rather than aligning single-agent outputs.
2. **PersonaEvolve (PEvo)**: An LLM-based optimizer that iteratively evolves agent personas, using emergent-behavior-versus-benchmark gaps as the feedback signal and an optimizer LLM as the update operator, so the crowd's collective behavior converges toward expert references.
3. **High-Stakes Crowd Simulation Evaluation**: Demonstrates that evolving personas improves behavioral realism of LLM agent crowds in high-stakes scenarios measured against expert benchmarks, without fine-tuning the base model.

## Relevance to Agent Evolution

PEvo is a persona/prompt-evolution loop: the mutable object is the agents' natural-language personas, the feedback signal is the divergence between emergent crowd behavior and expert benchmarks, and an optimizer LLM proposes and tests persona revisions. This sits alongside reflective prompt optimizers (e.g., GEPA) but evolves a population of personas to shape emergent multi-agent behavior, connecting persona/prompt evolution with population-level and evaluator-driven loops.
