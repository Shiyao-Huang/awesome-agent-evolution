\subsection{Benchmark Evidence for Self-Improvement Methods}
\label{sec:benchmark-self-improvement}

Table~\ref{tab:self-improvement-core-benchmarks} consolidates the benchmark numbers most directly used in the literature to justify language-model self-improvement claims.  The comparison separates prompt/context-level improvement (Reflexion, Self-Refine, Self-Debug) from weight-updating self-training (STaR, ReST$^{\mathrm{EM}}$, SPIN, Self-Rewarding).  A source discrepancy is important: the SPIN paper reports HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard and MT-Bench rather than AlpacaEval; AlpacaEval 2.0 numbers commonly associated with iterative self-play/self-reward training come from Self-Rewarding Language Models, not SPIN itself.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
The strongest evidence differs by mechanism.  Prompt-level and memory-level methods can deliver large test-time jumps when feedback is executable or task-grounded: Reflexion reports 91.0\% HumanEval pass@1 versus GPT-4's 80.1\%, and Self-Debug adds up to +13.1 pp on TransCoder.  Weight-updating loops deliver broader but slower improvements: SPIN raises the Open LLM Leaderboard average from 58.14 to 63.16 and GSM8K from 26.76 to 38.97, while ReST$^{\mathrm{EM}}$ improves PaLM-2 MATH/APPS pass@1 by roughly 6 pp.  The main caution is benchmark specificity: STaR's GSM8K result is directionally important but only 10.7\% absolute, and AlpacaEval gains should be attributed to Self-Rewarding/Meta-Rewarding style self-judging rather than to SPIN unless a source explicitly reports SPIN on AlpacaEval.
